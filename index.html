Turning a 27" 5K iMac into a 5K Display

The iMac's screen is the same 5120 x 2880 panel Apple uses in the Studio Display. We take the computer out, put in a display driver board, and the iMac becomes a full-resolution monitor for any Mac with USB-C or Thunderbolt.

First, check the iMac

This plan only fits a 27-inch iMac with Retina 5K display, 2014 to 2020 (model A1419 or A2115, printed under the stand). When the screen comes off, the sticker on the back of the panel should read LM270QQ1 or LM270QQ2. The board is ordered to match that sticker, so we read it before buying. Apple's Target Display Mode does not work on 5K iMacs, which is why the conversion needs new hardware.

What you keep and what you lose

Keep

  • Full 5K resolution at 60 Hz
  • The iMac's speakers, through small crossover filters
  • One cable from the Mac, with up to 65 W of laptop charging over USB-C
  • Brightness and volume from the Mac keyboard (free BetterDisplay app)
  • The original look: power cord in the original socket, cables out through the memory door
  • Extra inputs: DisplayPort and HDMI

Lose

  • The iMac as a computer
  • Built-in camera and microphones
  • SD card slot, headphone jack, Thunderbolt 2 ports
  • On some 2014-2015 panels, a faint pink tint along the edges may show up

What the job involves

  1. Open the iMac

    Unplug it and wait 30 minutes so the internal power supply drains. Cut the screen adhesive with an opening wheel and lift the glass panel off.

  2. Remove the computer

    Take out the logic board, Apple power supply, drive, and Wi-Fi antennas. Keep the speakers, the fan, and the power inlet cable.

  3. Bench test first

    Plug the panel's data and backlight cables into the new board and confirm a 5K picture from a Mac before anything gets mounted.

  4. Mount the board

    Fasten it on standoffs so its ports line up with the memory door. That door becomes the cable opening.

  5. Install the power supply

    A Mean Well 24 V supply sits inside the case. It is wired to the iMac's original power socket with a small connector and grounded to the case, so the stock power cord still works.

  6. Cooling and sound

    The iMac fan runs through a Noctua speed controller so it stays quiet. The speakers connect through a capacitor and coil on each side.

  7. Test, then seal

    Check picture, sound, charging and heat. Then apply new adhesive strips and press the glass back on.

Rough time: a basic board swap runs about 1 to 2 hours. The full build above (internal power supply, speakers, quiet fan) is closer to a full day, plus the wait for parts.

Things to know before deciding

  • This involves mains wiring inside the case. Done right it is safe; done wrong it is a shock and fire risk.
  • The glass panel is large and fragile, and replacements are expensive.
  • The R1820 board is new (February 2026). One owner reported its USB-C port failing after two months while HDMI kept working, so keep the box and receipt.
  • For comparison: a refurbished Apple Studio Display starts around $1,020, and a used LG UltraFine 5K runs about $449.
